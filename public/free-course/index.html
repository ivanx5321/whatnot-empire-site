Lesson 1 of 6

Start on the right platform

Start on Whatnot, not TikTok.

  • New TikTok shops get sales caps, category approvals, violations and payout holds. Whatnot lets you build your systems first.
  • Plan on about $10–15k to start. You don't need $100k. Most people wait for $100k and never go live.
  • Sell what you can source and what you'd enjoy talking about. Fast, cheap items with lots of variety (squishies, toys) beat clothes.
  • Expect 3–5 shows before a new account warms up. Keep going live on a consistent schedule.

Lesson 2 of 6

Buy inventory the right way

Go wide, not deep.

  • Buy 1–2 cases of each item and carry 100+ different SKUs. Buyers are collecting, and they always want something new.
  • Rotate between several suppliers so viewers rarely see the same item twice. Fresh product every week keeps your average up.
  • Know your landed cost per piece (total spend divided by units). If an item costs $4, it needs to sell for $6 minimum.
  • Never sell branded or character lookalikes. One IP strike can shut a shop down.

Lesson 3 of 6

Set up shipping so buyers keep buying

Shipping is where most new sellers lose the sale.

  • Cap what buyers pay so they keep bidding: "buy 2, next 8 ship free" or a flat pay-shipping-once rate.
  • Set realistic weights. Four small squishies weigh about 8–12 oz, not 2 lbs. Over-declared weight wastes money on every label.
  • Free shipping can lift weak hosts' averages a lot, but only if items are light and labor is tight. Once you switch, it's hard to go back.
  • Use number stickers: every sold item gets the next number, shelved in order. Two people can pack about 600 items in an hour.

Lesson 4 of 6

Run a show that sells

Grab and go.

  • Think about speed, not each item. 100 items an hour is the floor, and strong hosts do 130–200.
  • Sell one item at a time, pinned. Say "last one" to create urgency.
  • Set the camera so the product fills the frame. Prices go up the moment the angle is right.
  • Use timers with extensions (7+5 or 5+5). Short sudden death with no extension tanks prices on a small audience.

Lesson 5 of 6

Get more viewers

Viewers come from what you do before and during the live.

  • Post a short video right before every live. Videos always send traffic to the live.
  • Start a giveaway the second you go live, and push it hard in the first 5–10 minutes.
  • Avoid the crowded 4pm slot when you're new. Overnight, early morning and weekends are easier to win.
  • Consistency snowballs: a steady show can add about 30 new returning buyers a day.
Lesson 6 of 6

Know your real profit

Revenue isn't profit.

  • TikTok GMV includes the shipping buyers paid. A $6 average with free shipping is really about $4.50 an item.
  • A good baseline is about $600 an hour for one host (100 items at $6). Strong hosts do $1,000–1,200.
  • Track profit per stream: sales, minus fees, shipping, cost of goods and labor.
  • Sell-through is where the money is. Turn inventory over fast and keep buying fresh product.
